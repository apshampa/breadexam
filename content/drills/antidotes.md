| Poison / Toxin / Drug Overdose | Specific Antidote | Mechanism of Action & Clinical Pearl | Tier |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Paracetamol (Acetaminophen)** | **N-Acetylcysteine (NAC)** | Replenishes hepatic glutathione reserves to neutralize toxic metabolite **NAPQI**. Most effective within 8-10 hours. | Must-Know |
| **Opioids (Morphine, Heroin, Codeine)** | **Naloxone (IV)** | Pure competitive μ, κ, δ opioid receptor antagonist. Short half-life (~60 min); watch for recurrent respiratory depression. | Must-Know |
| **Benzodiazepines (Diazepam, Alprazolam)** | **Flumazenil (IV)** | Competitive antagonist at the BZD recognition site on GABA-A receptor. Caution: can precipitate withdrawal seizures in chronic users. | Must-Know |
| **Organophosphates (Malathion, Parathion)** | **Atropine + Pralidoxime (2-PAM)** | Atropine blocks muscarinic receptors (titrate until secretions dry); Pralidoxime reactivates AChE before chemical aging occurs. | Must-Know |
| **Atropine / Belladonna Poisoning** | **Physostigmine (slow IV)** | Tertiary amine cholinesterase inhibitor that crosses blood-brain barrier to reverse central anticholinergic delirium. | Must-Know |
| **Heparin (Unfractionated)** | **Protamine Sulfate (IV)** | Strongly basic polycationic peptide that neutralizes acidic heparin by forming a stable, inactive salt complex. | Must-Know |
| **Warfarin (Oral Anticoagulant)** | **Vitamin K1 (Phytonadione) + FFP / PCC** | Vitamin K restores synthesis of factors II, VII, IX, X; Fresh Frozen Plasma or 4-Factor PCC provides immediate replacement. | Must-Know |
| **Dabigatran (Direct Thrombin Inhibitor)** | **Idarucizumab** | Humanized monoclonal antibody fragment (Fab) that binds free and thrombin-bound dabigatran with 350x higher affinity than thrombin. | Should-Know |
| **Factor Xa Inhibitors (Apixaban, Rivaroxaban)** | **Andexanet Alfa** | Recombinant modified human factor Xa decoy protein that binds and sequesters Xa inhibitors. | Should-Know |
| **Digoxin / Digitalis Toxicity** | **Digoxin-specific Fab fragments (DigiFab)** | Specific antigen-binding fragments bind free digoxin; also give KCl if hypokalemic, and Phenytoin/Lignocaine for ventricular arrhythmias. | Must-Know |
| **Beta-Blocker Overdose** | **IV Glucagon (1st line)** | Stimulates adenylate cyclase via glucagon receptors bypassing beta-receptors, increasing cAMP and myocardial contractility/HR. | Must-Know |
| **Calcium Channel Blocker (CCB) Overdose** | **IV Calcium Gluconate + High-Dose Insulin Euglycemia (HIET)** | Restores intracellular calcium gradients; insulin enhances myocardial carbohydrate utilization and inotropic support. | Must-Know |
| **Iron Toxicity (Acute)** | **Desferrioxamine (Deferoxamine)** | Specific iron chelator; binds free ferric (Fe3+) iron forming stable ferrioxamine complex, excreted in urine producing classic "vin rose" color. | Must-Know |
| **Copper Toxicity / Wilson's Disease** | **D-Penicillamine / Trientine** | Chelates copper to form soluble complexes excreted in urine; give with Pyridoxine (B6) to prevent penicillamine-induced deficiency. | Must-Know |
| **Lead Poisoning (Plumbism)** | **Calcium Disodium EDTA + Dimercaprol (BAL)** | Chelates divalent lead; Dimercaprol crosses BBB for encephalopathy, EDTA clears circulating lead without depleting body calcium. | Must-Know |
| **Arsenic & Mercury Poisoning** | **Dimercaprol (BAL - British Anti-Lewisite)** | Competing sulfhydryl (-SH) donor that prevents heavy metal from binding tissue enzymes; alternative: oral Succimer (DMSA). | Must-Know |
| **Carbon Monoxide (CO)** | **100% Normobaric Oxygen / Hyperbaric O2** | Competitively displaces CO from hemoglobin (decreases carboxyhemoglobin half-life from 300 min on room air down to ~20 min in HBO). | Must-Know |
| **Cyanide Poisoning** | **Hydroxocobalamin / Nitrite-Thiosulfate kit** | Hydroxocobalamin binds cyanide to form non-toxic Cyanocobalamin (B12); Amyl/Sodium nitrite induces Methemoglobinemia which binds CN. | Must-Know |
| **Methanol / Ethylene Glycol** | **Fomepizole / Ethanol (10%)** | Competitively inhibits Alcohol Dehydrogenase (ADH), preventing formation of toxic metabolites (Formic acid / Oxalic acid). | Must-Know |
| **Isoniazid (INH) Overdose / Toxicity** | **Pyridoxine (Vitamin B6)** | INH depletes pyridoxal phosphate and inhibits GABA synthesis causing intractable seizures; Pyridoxine restores GABA. | Must-Know |
| **Methotrexate (Folate Antagonist)** | **Leucovorin (Folinic Acid) / Glucarpidase** | Provides active reduced folate bypassing blocked Dihydrofolate Reductase (DHFR) enzyme ("Leucovorin Rescue"). | Must-Know |
| **Cyclophosphamide / Ifosfamide Bladder Toxicity** | **Mesna (Sodium 2-mercaptoethane sulfonate)** | Inactivates urotoxic metabolite **Acrolein** in the bladder by forming a non-toxic thioether adduct, preventing hemorrhagic cystitis. | Must-Know |
| **Cisplatin Nephrotoxicity** | **Amifostine + Vigorous Hydration** | Free-radical scavenger that concentrates selectively in normal renal tubules before cisplatin administration. | Should-Know |
| **Doxorubicin Cardiotoxicity** | **Dexrazoxane** | Iron chelator that prevents iron-dependent free radical generation and cardiomyocyte apoptosis. | Should-Know |
| **Sulfonylurea-Induced Hypoglycemia** | **Octreotide + IV Dextrose** | Somatostatin analog that suppresses insulin hypersecretion from pancreatic beta cells. | Should-Know |
| **Serotonin Syndrome** | **Cyproheptadine** | Potent 5-HT2A receptor antagonist along with active cooling and benzodiazepines for muscle rigidity. | Should-Know |
| **Neuroleptic Malignant Syndrome (NMS)** | **Dantrolene + Bromocriptine** | Dantrolene inhibits ryanodine receptor (RyR1) Ca2+ release in skeletal muscle; Bromocriptine is a dopamine D2 agonist. | Must-Know |
| **Malignant Hyperthermia (Halothane/Suxamethonium)** | **Dantrolene Sodium (IV)** | Directly blocks ryanodine receptor Ca2+ release channels in the sarcoplasmic reticulum, stopping runaway muscle contracture. | Must-Know |
| **Tricyclic Antidepressant (TCA) Cardiotoxicity** | **IV Sodium Bicarbonate (NaHCO3)** | Overcomes fast sodium channel blockade (narrows wide QRS) and induces blood alkalinization to decrease free drug fraction. | Must-Know |
| **Local Anesthetic Toxicity (LAST)** | **20% Intralipid (Lipid Emulsion)** | "Lipid sink" theory: sequesters lipophilic local anesthetics (Bupivacaine) from myocardial tissue and enhances fatty acid metabolism. | Should-Know |
