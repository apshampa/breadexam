# Antimicrobials 1: Penicillins, Cephalosporins & Protein Synthesis Inhibitors

### 📖 References
- **Shanbhag (4th Ed):** Shanbhag Ch 39 - 43 (pp. 317-356)
- **KD Tripathi (Essentials):** KD Tripathi Section 12 (Ch 51 - 54)

---

## 🎯 Today's Core Learning Objectives
- [ ] Penicillins: Classification, Penicillin G vs extended spectrum (Amoxicillin, Piperacillin), Anaphylaxis management
- [ ] Beta-lactamase inhibitors: Clavulanic acid, Sulbactam, Tazobactam mechanism of suicide inhibition
- [ ] Cephalosporins: 1st to 5th generations, anti-pseudomonal coverage (Ceftazidime, Cefepime), and BBB penetration (Ceftriaxone)
- [ ] Aminoglycosides (Gentamicin, Amikacin): Mechanism (30S), Ototoxicity (vestibular/cochlear), Nephrotoxicity, once-daily dosing rationale
- [ ] Macrolides (Azithromycin, Clarithromycin) & Tetracyclines (Doxycycline): Spectrum and classic adverse effects
- [ ] Chloramphenicol: Gray baby syndrome and bone marrow aplasia

---

## 💡 High-Yield Exam Pearls (Must-Memorize)
> ⚡ Buy AT 30, CELL at 50: Aminoglycosides & Tetracyclines bind 30S; Chloramphenicol, Erythromycin (Macrolides), Clindamycin, Linezolid bind 50S.
>
> ⚡ Aminoglycosides exhibit Concentration-Dependent Killing and Post-Antibiotic Effect (PAE), justifying once-daily high-dose dosing.
>
> ⚡ Vancomycin causes 'Red Man Syndrome' via direct non-IgE mast cell histamine release if infused too rapidly (prevented by slow IV infusion over >60 min).
>
> ⚡ Doxycycline is excreted primarily via bile/feces; it is the safest tetracycline in renal failure.
