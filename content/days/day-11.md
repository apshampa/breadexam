# Endocrine 1: Diabetes Mellitus & Corticosteroids

### 📖 References
- **Shanbhag (4th Ed):** Shanbhag Ch 34, 38 (pp. 265-280, 305-316)
- **KD Tripathi (Essentials):** KD Tripathi Section 5 (Ch 19 - 22)

---

## 🎯 Today's Core Learning Objectives
- [ ] Insulin: Rapid-acting (Lispro, Aspart), Short-acting (Regular), Intermediate (NPH), and Long-acting basal analogs (Glargine, Degludec)
- [ ] Metformin: First-line biguanide, AMPK activation, lack of hypoglycemia, risk of lactic acidosis
- [ ] Sulfonylureas (Glimepiride) vs Meglitinides: K-ATP channel blockade
- [ ] Modern Antidiabetics: SGLT2 inhibitors (Dapagliflozin), DPP-4 inhibitors (Sitagliptin), GLP-1 receptor agonists (Semaglutide)
- [ ] Glucocorticoids: Anti-inflammatory & immunosuppressive MOA (lipocortin/annexin A1, NF-kB suppression)
- [ ] Adverse effects of long-term steroids (Cushingoid state, osteoporosis, hyperglycemia, HPA axis suppression) and safe tapering rules

---

## 💡 High-Yield Exam Pearls (Must-Memorize)
> ⚡ Regular (Crystalline) Insulin is the ONLY insulin that can be given intravenously (used in DKA).
>
> ⚡ SGLT2 inhibitors (-gliflozins): Promote glucosuria by blocking SGLT2 in renal proximal tubule; proven cardiovascular & renal mortality benefits; risk of mycotic genital infections and euglycemic DKA.
>
> ⚡ Corticosteroid withdrawal: Prolonged use causes bilateral adrenocortical atrophy; abrupt stoppage triggers fatal Addisonian crisis. Must taper slowly.
