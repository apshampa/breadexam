## 🎯 Today's Core Learning Objectives
- [ ] Peptic Ulcer: Proton Pump Inhibitors (Omeprazole, Pantoprazole) irreversible H+/K+ ATPase inhibition vs H2 blockers (Ranitidine)
- [ ] Triple Drug Regimen for Helicobacter pylori eradication (PPI + Clarithromycin + Amoxicillin / Metronidazole)
- [ ] Antiemetics: 5-HT3 antagonists (Ondansetron), D2 antagonists (Metoclopramide, Domperidone), NK1 antagonists (Aprepitant)
- [ ] Prokinetics: Metoclopramide mechanism and extrapyramidal adverse effects (acute dystonia)
- [ ] Heavy Metal Poisoning & Chelating Agents: BAL / Dimercaprol (Arsenic, Mercury), Calcium disodium EDTA (Lead), D-Penicillamine (Copper / Wilson's), Desferrioxamine (Iron)

---

## 💡 High-Yield Exam Pearls (Must-Memorize)
> ⚡ PPIs are prodrugs: Enteric-coated to prevent premature activation by gastric acid; absorbed in intestine, reach parietal canaliculi via bloodstream, activated by acid to sulfenamide form which binds H+/K+ ATPase covalently.
>
> ⚡ Domperidone does not cross blood-brain barrier; unlike Metoclopramide, it does NOT cause extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS).
>
> ⚡ Acute Iron poisoning in children: Causes hemorrhagic gastroenteritis and shock; treated with IV Desferrioxamine (turns urine 'vin rose').
