# CNS 2 & Autacoids: Opioids, NSAIDs, Gout & Antipsychotics

### 📖 References
- **Shanbhag (4th Ed):** Shanbhag Ch 18 - 22 (pp. 139-164)
- **KD Tripathi (Essentials):** KD Tripathi Section 3 (Ch 13 - 16) & Section 6 (Ch 32 - 35)

---

## 🎯 Today's Core Learning Objectives
- [ ] Morphine: Opioid receptor subtypes (μ, κ, δ), actions, adverse effects, and life-threatening contraindications
- [ ] Acute Opioid Overdose triad (Coma, Pinpoint pupils, Respiratory depression) & Naloxone therapy
- [ ] Aspirin: Irreversible COX-1/2 inhibition, antiplatelet dose vs anti-inflammatory dose, Reye's syndrome, Salicylism
- [ ] Paracetamol (Acetaminophen) hepatotoxicity: NAPQI pathway and N-Acetylcysteine protocol
- [ ] Gout pharmacology: Acute attack management (NSAIDs/Colchicine) vs Chronic hypouricemic therapy (Allopurinol/Febuxostat)
- [ ] Typical vs Atypical Antipsychotics: Extrapyramidal symptoms (EPS) vs Metabolic syndrome; Clozapine and Agranulocytosis

---

## 💡 High-Yield Exam Pearls (Must-Memorize)
> ⚡ Morphine contraindications: Head injury, Bronchial asthma, Undiagnosed acute abdomen, BPH, Biliary colic.
>
> ⚡ Paracetamol toxicity antidote: N-Acetylcysteine (IV or oral) within 8-10 hours replenishes hepatic glutathione.
>
> ⚡ Clozapine: Most effective atypical antipsychotic for treatment-resistant schizophrenia; causes agranulocytosis (requires weekly ANC monitoring) and seizures.
>
> ⚡ Colchicine: Inhibits microtubule assembly/tubulin polymerization in neutrophils, blocking inflammatory migration.
