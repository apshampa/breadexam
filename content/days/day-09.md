# Antimicrobials 2: Fluoroquinolones, Anti-TB & Antifungals

### 📖 References
- **Shanbhag (4th Ed):** Shanbhag Ch 44 - 47 (pp. 357-392)
- **KD Tripathi (Essentials):** KD Tripathi Section 12 (Ch 55 - 58)

---

## 🎯 Today's Core Learning Objectives
- [ ] Fluoroquinolones (Ciprofloxacin, Levofloxacin): MOA (DNA gyrase / Topoisomerase IV), tendonitis/tendon rupture, and cartilage toxicity
- [ ] First-line Anti-Tubercular Drugs (HRZES regimen): Mechanisms, bactericidal vs bacteriostatic, and toxicities
- [ ] Isoniazid (INH): Hepatotoxicity, peripheral neuropathy and Pyridoxine protection
- [ ] Rifampicin: RNA polymerase inhibition, red-orange discoloration of secretions, potent CYP450 induction
- [ ] Pyrazinamide (hyperuricemia) & Ethambutol (retrobulbar neuritis)
- [ ] Antifungals: Amphotericin B ('Ampho-terrible' nephrotoxicity & liposomal formulation) vs Azoles (Ergosterol synthesis inhibition)

---

## 💡 High-Yield Exam Pearls (Must-Memorize)
> ⚡ First-line TB drugs: **H** (Isoniazid), **R** (Rifampicin), **Z** (Pyrazinamide), **E** (Ethambutol), **S** (Streptomycin).
>
> ⚡ All 1st-line TB drugs are bactericidal EXCEPT Ethambutol (bacteriostatic).
>
> ⚡ All 1st-line TB drugs are hepatotoxic EXCEPT Ethambutol and Streptomycin.
>
> ⚡ Amphotericin B binds Ergosterol in fungal membrane forming pores; Azoles inhibit 14α-demethylase preventing ergosterol synthesis.
